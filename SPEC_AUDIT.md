## 1. Result and scope

The supplied theory stack supports a distinct, bounded source-lineage audit project. Its source-derived center is continuing external presence, preservation of provenance and epistemic status, visibility of common ancestry, appropriate validation, preservation of anomalies and usable corrective capacity. The project’s exact graph predicates, root counts, assurance classes, concentration rules and observability levels require toolkit operationalization. The papers do not supply these as a finished software specification. [SIL, pp. 7 and 11-16; UIL, pp. 5 and 8-9; EC, §§6-7, pp. 9-11; HDL, §§9-11, pp. 22-29]

This audit identifies two source-to-software interpretation holds that require explicit owner decisions: the meaning and direction of entropy-related diagnostics, and the treatment of synthetic judgment relative to human corrective input. It also records the engineering decisions needed before a later unit can freeze data or reporting semantics. None is silently settled by a recommended option.

The current result is **ready for Work Unit 1 review**. It is not a Phase 0 pass or an implementation-readiness certificate. Consumer specifications remain paused at their recorded gates.

## 4. Source-by-source audit

### 4.1 SIL: primary source-governance architecture

**Source-derived content.** SIL places source integrity upstream of answer generation and defines Presence and Integrity in its own terms. Presence includes broad and continuing contact with actual people, institutions, local realities, minority experience, edge cases and expertise. Integrity includes provenance, accountability, auditable process, resistance to manipulation and conflict disclosure. The paper requires both. [SIL, pp. 6-7 and 11-12]

The source contains three different organizational structures that must remain distinguishable:

| Source organization | Terms or components | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| Three data layers | Surface-linguistic; world-model; judgment | pp. 6-7 |
| Four governance layers | Open Web; Verified; Contested; Judgment | p. 13, table |
| Seven architecture components | Open registry; provenance; trust-weighted retrieval; manipulation detection; human audit nodes; source return; appeals | pp. 12-15 |

The table on p. 13 supplies a contribution, failure mode and governance requirement for each of the four layers. It does not supply a total ranking, a disjoint enum or a source-truth classification. For example, the Verified row includes its own risks of gatekeeping and a false aura of truth; the Contested row calls for visible disagreement and disclosed conflicts.

**Direct product relevance.** The source supports preserving source identity, verification scope, editorial and revision history, human or machine involvement, declared conflicts, correction behavior and review routes. Its provenance paragraph also names signatures or checksums and original-versus-syndicated status. [SIL, pp. 13-15]

**Required separation.** The full layer would operate retrieval, sanitation, review institutions and economic return. A toolkit that audits supplied provenance records covers only part of that architecture. Deferral of a registry or payment system is a product boundary, not evidence that the source considers those functions optional. [SIL, pp. 14-16; PLAN, §4]

**Material interpretation issue.** SIL p. 6 characterizes model-generated judgment as reinforcing inherited framings and uses the phrase “actively poisonous at the third.” SIL p. 7 subsequently states a conditional replacement-versus-accumulation account and says the problem concerns loss of fresh, real and heterogeneous input. Its later human-judgment requirement remains explicit. These passages cannot be translated into either a blanket synthetic-source rejection or an unrestricted synthetic-judge qualification without an owner decision. See SIT-A002 and SIT-D002.

**Uninspected dependencies.** SIL cites *Data as Structure in the Age of AI* and *Intelligence as a Dissipative Structure*. Their complete texts are not in the six-file input set. This unit attributes the summarized arguments to SIL, rather than pretending to have audited those originals. The project need not obtain every cited work to record SIL’s own statements; a later claim requiring the original derivation must obtain and inspect that source.

### 4.2 UIL: structural law and finite-resampling core

**Source-derived content.** UIL defines closed recursion by generation from prior internal outputs under insufficient external difference or correction. It links closure to diversity contraction, inherited deviation, correlated error and declining fidelity to an external reference. It explicitly separates an exact stochastic core from broader domain-specific structural extensions. [UIL, pp. 2-5 and 9-10; pp. 15-16]

Its compact dependencies are `Q = I × D` and `S = P × I`. Here Q denotes quality, D diversity, P external presence, I integrity and S stability. The paper says these are structural dependencies rather than a complete dynamical model. [UIL, p. 5]

**Formal content as presented.** In the stated finite model, a generation is reconstructed from n multinomial samples of the current distribution. With Gini-Simpson diversity `D_t = 1 - sum_i p_(t,i)^2`, the paper states:

- `E[D_(t+1) | p_t] = (1 - 1/n) D_t`;
- `Pr(p_(t+1,i) = 0 | p_(t,i)) = (1 - p_(t,i))^n`;
- an absent state cannot return through closed resampling;
- for `L_t = ||p_t - q||_2^2`, `E[L_(t+1) | p_t] = L_t + D_t/n`.

The source reopens the model by mixing the current distribution with an external distribution before sampling. It warns that input strength alone is insufficient when the external distribution is contaminated, unrepresentative or derived from the same closed source. These statements are recorded from the displayed equations and prose, without running a simulation or porting them to source graphs. [UIL, pp. 6-9]

**Direct product relevance.** Source identity cannot substitute for genuine external difference. Repeated artifacts can inherit one evidentiary origin. New timestamps do not eliminate that ancestry. These are reasons to preserve lineage and the origin of corrective input. [UIL, pp. 8-9 and 15-17]

**Boundary.** UIL explicitly allows functional convergence when the relevant task has a correct stable optimum or no continuing need for eliminated variation. A concentrated source set can therefore be described accurately without declaring the underlying claim false or the system collapsed. [UIL, pp. 9 and 16]

**Missing operational bridge.** The paper does not define this toolkit’s independent-root equivalence classes, root weights, unknown-root count or confidence interval. Those cannot be imported from the resampling formulas by substituting documents for states or graph depth for generations.

### 4.3 EC: evaluator ancestry, validity and correction

**Source-derived content.** EC defines evaluation closure and benchmark inbreeding, distinguishes measurement, distributional and structural validity, and treats the benchmark cut as an upstream decision. It specifically describes common model families, reference generators, rubric conventions and judges as possible channels of shared error. [EC, §§1-3, pp. 2-5]

**Reopening.** Distributional reopening introduces cases within a represented state space. Structural reopening changes that space when preserved anomalies expose an inadequate cut. Refreshing examples does not establish structural reopening. [EC, §4.4-4.5, pp. 7-8]

**Validation.** EC requires different constraints for formal, empirical, causal, coding, agentic, safety and human-consequence claims. This prevents a source-origin audit from pretending that all support must come from separate eyewitness observations or that all validation can come from a model judge. [EC, §6, pp. 9-10]

**Architecture.** EC’s five conditions are structural validity, external presence, source integrity, tail retention and corrective capacity. The displayed expression on p. 11 joins them with logical conjunction. The S in this expression denotes structural validity, whereas UIL’s S denotes stability. These uses must not be merged by symbol matching. [EC, §7, pp. 10-11]

**Record requirement.** EC identifies content, source, knowledge type, transformation history, model lineage, validation status and unresolved uncertainty as the durable evaluation record. It describes corrective capacity through actual powers such as escalation, rollback, restriction, revision or withdrawal. [EC, §7.3-7.5, p. 11]

**Boundary.** The paper’s provenance-graph research proposal supports investigating shared ancestry. It supplies no formula turning model-family overlap into an observed error correlation. It also retains traditional benchmarks as narrow probes. The toolkit should audit declared evidence and evaluative relations without claiming to replace all benchmarking. [EC, §§9 and 11-12, pp. 12-14]

### 4.4 HDL: reality relation, transformation and effective replenishment

**Source-derived content.** HDL requires a declared reality variable and distinguishes surface entropy from mutual information with that variable. It defines semantic gradient through state-conditioned Jensen-Shannon separation and requires a fixed pair weighting for a comparison. These are different quantities with different inputs. [HDL, §3, pp. 5-8]

**Formal boundary.** Its data-processing result depends on a closed Markov channel. Its strict decay bound requires an additional strict-contraction condition. Its decision-theoretic comparison concerns the attainable information value of experiments and does not imply that every bounded model or task worsens after compression. The source also allows incomparable experiments and new side information. [HDL, §4, pp. 8-13; §11, pp. 28-29]

**Replenishment.** Page 13 identifies four conditions for effective replenishment: independent reality-bearing difference enters; preprocessing preserves it; selection gives it sufficient weight; and it affects downstream training, retrieval or generation. This makes it essential to separate storage admission from demonstrated downstream influence. [HDL, §4.6, p. 13]

**Architecture.** Section 9 presents nine functions: functional crawler identity, open source registration, minimum provenance, epistemic-status preservation, contestable trust weighting, manipulation detection, anomaly retention, audit and appeal, and source return. Section 10.5 states that the diagnostic profile should not collapse into one score. [HDL, pp. 22-27]

**Measurement boundary.** The source warns about high-dimensional estimation, demands independently measured reality records, requires held-out event-level evaluation and fixed representations, and distinguishes empirical diagnostics from theorem statements. A lineage graph does not automatically provide those measurement conditions. [HDL, §§10.1-10.4, pp. 24-26]

**Direct product relevance.** Preserved epistemic status, claim-specific interpretation, visible source transformation, external-input qualification and correction evidence can guide the toolkit. Direct measurement of semantic gradient, mutual information, half-life or causal correction elasticity requires a separate experimental scope.

### 4.5 EBC: background boundary condition and a terminology hold

**Source-derived content.** EBC distinguishes physical or informational mechanisms from entropy as a descriptive measure or boundary. Its added derivation discussion explicitly limits the physical example by model, scaling and regularity assumptions. [EBC, pp. 1 and 3-4; pp. 7-9]

**Material translation issue.** The AI discussion on pp. 5-6 describes increasing statistical entropy and an eventual maximum-entropy random-output state. UIL’s finite core describes support concentration, and HDL expressly separates output entropy from reality-coupled information. A universal software variable named entropy cannot receive all these roles at once. The passages remain recorded as written; this audit does not revise them or assert that their general usages are numerically identical. See SIT-A001 and SIT-D001.

**Recommended limited use, pending decision.** Keep EBC in the already planned background role. Import the distinction between causes and descriptions; do not construct a diagnostic from its general AI entropy language. This is a proposed product handling of the source difference, not a correction to the paper.

**Metadata and extraction.** The cover copyright and revision note give different year-related metadata. Several mathematical or citation fragments are imperfectly represented in the text stream. The exact file hash remains the authoritative audit identifier. Neither a publication date nor a missing citation is silently reconstructed.

### 4.6 BVL: adjacent guidance for correction boundaries

**Source-derived content.** BVL separates accessibility and routed capture from sustainable carrying capacity. It gives distinct weighted-flow, absorbing-state, queueing, cut and cascade models with stated assumptions. Its human-oversight discussion warns that a nominal review role can lose practical effect when its load exceeds actual capacity. [BVL, pp. 7-15 and 22-25]

**Narrow project relevance.** This helps identify an overclaim in equating a reachable correction node with effective correction. A real route also depends on applicable authority, timing, processing and outcome evidence. [BVL, pp. 22-23; EC, §7.5, p. 11]

**Boundary.** BVL’s P denotes capture accessibility, not the Presence of SIL or UIL. Its graph equations do not become source-independence formulas. No broad social classification, capture probability, queue model or cascade engine is imported into v0.1.

## 6. Findings and decision gates

Finding IDs name audit observations. Decision IDs name the owner choices that must resolve them. Recommendations are recorded in `UNRESOLVED_DECISIONS.md`; the finding register does not enact them.

### SIT-A001: Entropy diagnostics would merge different quantities

**Evidence.** EBC pp. 5-6 uses a rising-output-entropy account. UIL pp. 6-9 gives finite support concentration and diversity contraction. HDL §3-4, pp. 6-13, separates surface entropy, mutual information and semantic gradient and distinguishes non-expansion from strict decay.

**Engineering consequence.** Any generic entropy value, failure threshold or sign rule would silently select or mix meanings. The plan already limits EBC to background and prohibits an entropy score, but the source treatment must still be explicit before dependent claims are written.

**Disposition.** Source-to-software interpretation hold, SIT-D001. No entropy diagnostic is specified.

### SIT-A002: Synthetic judgment cannot receive an unexamined blanket rule

**Evidence.** SIL p. 6 makes the strong judgment-layer claim; p. 7 gives a conditional replenishment account; pp. 14-16 require renewable human judgment. UIL pp. 11-12 and HDL §5.5 retain external-constraint distinctions, while EC §6 assigns validation by claim type. PLAN §5, Rule 4, rejects automatic integrity from source type.

**Engineering consequence.** Neither automatic rejection of all synthetic artifacts nor qualification of synthetic judges as independent human correction follows from the complete source set. The product must preserve the judgment-layer claim without erasing formal, empirical and provenance distinctions.

**Disposition.** Source/plan interpretation hold, SIT-D002. The audit maps the passages and pauses affected admissibility semantics.

### SIT-A003: Auditing a supplied graph and operating the entire source layer are different scopes

**Evidence.** SIL’s seven components and HDL’s nine functions include functions beyond this toolkit’s initial boundary. PLAN §§3-4 proposes a local-first analytical product and excludes crawlers, registries, ranking systems and payment infrastructure.

**Engineering consequence.** The first workflow must state what the user supplies and what the toolkit can derive. Model-based discovery of missing ancestry would create an additional evidentiary and security problem.

**Disposition.** SIT-D003 must select the first-domain slice before Work Unit 2.

### SIT-A004: Artifact, actor, observation and validation origin cannot share one overloaded identity

**Evidence.** SIL p. 14 names authors, publishers and original-versus-syndicated status. EC §6 distinguishes validation types. HDL §3.1 requires a declared inquiry. PLAN §5, Rule 1, requires claim-relative independence.

**Engineering consequence.** A publisher can issue a derivative report about an event and an original statement about its own publication decision. Reanalysis of common data can add analytical difference without adding a new collection event. A mixed artifact can contain both original and derivative evidence.

**Disposition.** SIT-D004, before the ontology is frozen.

### SIT-A005: No observed common ancestor is insufficient evidence of independence

**Evidence.** EC §3.2 and §11 identify shared lineages; HDL §4.6 requires genuine independent difference. No supplied source gives a universal statistical independence test for partial provenance graphs.

**Engineering consequence.** Independence needs a scope, dependency dimensions, comparison set and evidence standard. Different publishers can share a measurement; independent measurements can share a publisher. Distinct endpoints can share a model family. A common observed event alone is not necessarily a shared reporting source.

**Disposition.** SIT-D005. Do not infer transitive or mutual statistical independence from disconnected graph components.

### SIT-A006: Unknown root count can imply knowledge the input does not contain

**Evidence.** The plan correctly prohibits treating missing ancestry as independence, while also proposing an Unknown Root Count. The papers do not define how hidden upstream multiplicity should be counted.

**Engineering consequence.** Three unresolved parent references might conceal one shared origin or many origins. Reporting three unknown roots would appear more precise than the observed information warrants. Renormalizing only resolved ancestry can also hide uncertainty.

**Disposition.** SIT-D006. Recommend a distinct unresolved-boundary representation, with any numeric root claim restricted to an explicitly justified set.

### SIT-A007: Graph edges can carry incompatible meanings and conflicting assertions

**Evidence.** EC §7.3 and HDL §9.4 preserve knowledge type, uncertainty and transformation history. The plan’s candidate relations include citation, derivation, support, validation, correction and publication.

**Engineering consequence.** A citation is not automatically evidentiary support. A support assertion does not certify truth. An ownership relation is not a content-copy relation. Conflicting edge claims and versioned artifacts must remain inspectable. Citation cycles and impossible temporal derivation cycles need different treatment.

**Disposition.** SIT-D007. Exact edge names, directions and validation rules remain open.

### SIT-A008: A snapshot cannot measure effective ongoing presence

**Evidence.** EC §7.2 makes Presence temporal and causal. HDL §4.6 requires entry, retention, selection and downstream influence.

**Engineering consequence.** Ingestion timestamps cannot stand in for observation dates, externality or actual effect. Stored but excluded evidence may establish admission without replenishment. Archived original evidence can be relevant to a historical claim without being a fresh observation.

**Disposition.** SIT-D008. The future report must distinguish evidence of each stage and avoid a universal freshness threshold.

### SIT-A009: Correction reachability can overstate corrective capacity

**Evidence.** EC §7.5 concerns actual powers to change the system. HDL §10.5 proposes observing correction speed and degree. BVL pp. 22-23 adds the capacity boundary.

**Engineering consequence.** A route can exist without permission, applicability, processing or downstream action. Missing paths in an incomplete graph remain unresolved. An incoming objection may be invalid, and rejection with reasons can be a functioning correction process. A changed answer alone does not prove the objection caused the change.

**Disposition.** SIT-D009. Separate route evidence from authority and observed outcomes.

### SIT-A010: The proposed metrics have no approved units or calibration yet

**Evidence.** PLAN §11 labels candidate metrics and requires units, denominators, missing-data rules and interpretation. HDL §10.5 requires a profile. UIL pp. 9 and 16 limits failure interpretations of concentration.

**Engineering consequence.** Nominal count, independent-root count, derivative share, HHI and effective count would answer different questions. Root weights must specify how multi-origin items contribute. Labels such as high or extreme have no approved threshold. Removing uncertain entries must not silently improve an apparent assurance claim.

**Disposition.** SIT-D010. No concentration formula, normalized score, threshold or golden number is frozen in this unit.

### SIT-A011: Source layers must not become an unsupported credibility ladder

**Evidence.** SIL p. 13 is a four-row governance table; SIL pp. 6-7 and HDL §2 discuss different three-layer structures.

**Engineering consequence.** A verified source can be contested and can supply judgment. The source table does not decide overlap or exclusivity. Combining data layers, source functions and verification states into one enum would lose distinctions.

**Disposition.** SIT-D011. Retain the original terms and decide their software representation explicitly.

### SIT-A012: Observability cannot serve as a quality score or a universal prerequisite ladder

**Evidence.** PLAN §13 proposes Levels 0-4 together with a capability matrix. EC §10 and HDL §10 require disclosure of evidentiary limits.

**Engineering consequence.** A highly observed process can be highly dependent. A correction event may be observable despite unresolved root ancestry. A low numbered level must not hide separately supported findings or turn unknown information into a pass.

**Disposition.** SIT-D012. Decide the relationship between descriptive levels and field-specific prerequisites.

### SIT-A013: Retention and suppression require a comparison universe

**Evidence.** EC §7.1 and §7.4 and HDL §9.7 preserve anomalies and rare information. The plan proposes tail and contestation retention.

**Engineering consequence.** A final set alone cannot reveal all discarded or uncollected evidence. Disagreement is a relation to a claim under a context, not a global source defect. Anomalies may need to remain unclassified to challenge the current taxonomy.

**Disposition.** SIT-D013. Represent supplied evidence and documented selection changes; withhold global retention rates without a denominator.

### SIT-A014: Protected identities and untrusted metadata need explicit handling

**Evidence.** SIL requires accountability and auditability while retaining non-verified sources. PLAN §16 requires privacy and safe handling of untrusted input. The theory supplies no pseudonymous identity or secret-handling protocol.

**Engineering consequence.** A required public name could exclude protected sources. Missing private metadata must not become independent provenance, and redaction must not erase known dependence. Claimed origin or an embedded instruction cannot become an instruction to the toolkit.

**Disposition.** SIT-D014. Privacy mechanisms and input limits remain proposed, for later detailed specification.

### SIT-A015: Shared theory does not establish exclusive module ownership or a shared runtime

**Evidence.** PLAN §3.3 establishes separate projects and §17 prohibits dependence on Recursive Integrity Toolkit internals. It lists lineage-related responsibilities for this toolkit.

**Engineering consequence.** Those lists define this product’s focus; they do not prohibit another toolkit from using provenance techniques. A future interchange requires explicit semantic mapping and missing-field handling. Existing theory-paper licenses do not select the new project’s code license.

**Disposition.** SIT-D015. No cross-project code, dependency, license adoption or repository write is authorized here.

These treatments do not remove source requirements from the theory map. They state the limits of what this particular product would claim.

## 8. Undefined terms that later specifications must operationalize

| Term | Source establishes | Still required for software |
|---|---|---|
| Independent source | Genuine external difference matters | Relationship scope, process dimensions, evidence basis and comparison set |
| Root source | Upstream origin and lineage matter | Root relative to which edge types, claim, time and stopping boundary |
| Observation | Reality-bearing input can reopen a system | Whether the origin is a collection event, testimony, formal artifact or another type-appropriate constraint |
| Source integrity | Provenance, accountability and correction matter | A bounded profile and evidence prerequisites; no invented universal scalar |
| External presence | Contact must be continuing and relevant | Boundary, time horizon and admission-to-effect evidence |
| Known / verified | Evidence can support identity and process | Who asserted what, how it was checked and which uncertainty remains |
| Corrective independence | Shared validation ancestry matters | Which evidence and process dependencies disqualify a specific independence claim |
| Correction reachability | Correction needs a real route to change | Applicable authority, graph coverage, processing conditions and event outcomes |
| Lineage concentration | Common ancestry can reduce apparent plurality | Weights, denominators, multi-parent allocation and unresolved contribution |
| Tail retention | Rare and anomalous evidence must remain available | Reference population, stage comparison and preservation of unclassified cases |
| Observability level | The plan proposes a coverage description | Capability prerequisites without a quality ranking |

Open terms are not reasons to fill gaps with an LLM’s general intuitions. They are the named decisions that make the theory-to-product boundary auditable.

## 9. Specification-level counterexamples

These examples are reasoning checks for this work unit. They are hypothetical and are not machine-readable fixtures or final golden outputs.

| Scenario | What the source-informed contract must preserve | Forbidden shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Five pages explicitly copy one measurement record | Five visible artifacts and one documented measurement origin | Five independent observations |
| Two sensors record the same event with separate collection records | Shared event plus separately documented acquisition, with any common calibration noted | Collapse the two solely because the event is the same |
| Two analysts examine one dataset | One data-collection lineage plus separately described analytical methods | Two independent datasets, or automatic absence of any analytical difference |
| One artifact copies a claim but adds a new interview relevant to another claim | Claim-specific roles and mixed ancestry | One global independent/derivative status |
| Three references have missing upstream history | Three observed unresolved references, with unknown origin multiplicity | Three independent roots |
| A judge and candidate share a documented base model | Known overlap, role and version | Measured correlated error, identical behavior or malicious coordination |
| New evidence enters storage but is excluded from every answer | Admission evidence and known selection exclusion | Effective replenishment established by ingestion alone |
| A complaint mailbox exists without power to revise the answer | Communication route and unknown or absent intervention authority | Full corrective capacity |
| An investigated objection is rejected with documented reasons | A review outcome whose validity can be audited | Automatic evidence of a failed correction channel |
| An anonymous source has a protected-origin attestation | Available assurance and explicit undisclosed dimensions | Worthless evidence or universal independence |
| The final bundle lacks a dissenting item, with no intake record | Incomplete coverage | Proven suppression |
| A lossless reformat preserves a source’s relevant content | Transformation and retained ancestry | Automatic semantic decay |

These checks motivate the pending decisions. They do not certify that an implementation has passed them.

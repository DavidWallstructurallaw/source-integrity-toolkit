## 1. Product statement

**Source Integrity Toolkit is a local-first, deterministic auditor of supplied, structured source and evidence records. It produces claim-scoped lineage findings and evidence-qualified profiles of external presence, evaluator dependence and correction.**

Its first useful workflow is an AI-answer or retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) evidence-bundle audit. An investigator supplies the claims, artifact versions, provenance assertions and available supporting records. Later software checks the structure of that bundle, derives permitted relationships from its typed graph, and reports what the supplied evidence supports and leaves unresolved.

The product answers questions such as:

- Which cited artifacts trace to the same documented origin for this claim?
- Which apparent sources contribute a separately documented observation or other type-appropriate constraint?
- What supports an independence assertion, and which dependency dimensions remain unexamined?
- Do a candidate, reference answer, rubric or evaluator share documented ancestry?
- Has external evidence merely entered storage, or is its preservation, selection or downstream use documented?
- Is a correction route declared, applicable and authorized, and is actual handling or downstream change evidenced?

A report remains conditional on the supplied records, the inquiry and the audited boundary. The software cannot certify undisclosed ancestry or turn a source inventory into a probability that the claim is true. [SIT-T008, SIT-T016, SIT-T018, SIT-T022, SIT-T029, SIT-T035-SIT-T040]

### 2.1 Source-local mathematical meanings

Under the accepted SIT-D001 direction, each quantity keeps its source, variables and assumptions. Thermodynamic entropy, output entropy, Gini-Simpson diversity, mutual information, semantic gradient, replenishment strength and queue utilization remain separate. Neither graph depth nor a missing-provenance fraction may be renamed one of those quantities.

This proposal supplies no empirical calibration, new scientific verification or reconciliation of the papers. Corporate case histories, public-web measurements and political examples in the papers are not used as calibration data or real-world labels for the toolkit.

### 2.2 Human judgment and other forms of validation

Under the accepted SIT-D002 direction, synthetic summaries and model judgments remain representable with disclosed ancestry. SIL's renewable human-judgment requirement is preserved. A model judge, a related-model review or a human-authored label alone cannot be counted as an independent human corrective contribution.

Substantive human review, formal checking, physical measurements and execution records retain their different roles. An execution record can constrain a coding claim without becoming a human judgment record. The toolkit records the method, contribution and evidence; it does not perform that external validation itself. [SIL, pp. 6-7, 14-16; EC, §6; HDL, §5.5]

### 2.3 Layer vocabularies

The three SIL data roles, surface-linguistic, world-model and judgment, are distinct from its four governance layers, Open Web, Verified, Contested and Judgment. HDL's world-structure and judgment-structure terminology remains visible in source attribution.

Accepted direction SIT-D011 permits separate axes or nonexclusive role assertions. That is a product modeling choice, not a software taxonomy already specified by the papers. No credibility ladder or automatic assignment from a publisher's name is introduced here.

## 3. Users and first-release workflow

### 3.1 Primary user

The primary user is an engineer, evaluator or research analyst who can assemble an explicit evidence dossier for an AI answer or RAG output. The dossier may be prepared manually or exported by another system, but its assertions must retain their author, method and supporting references.

The product does not require the user to operate a live registry, expose confidential source identities publicly, or connect a model provider account.

### 3.2 Adjacent supported workflows

Research citations, benchmark/reference provenance and dataset-source dossiers are supported only when they fit the same bounded record contract. These are applications of one audit model, not separate v0.1 products with dedicated crawlers, scoring engines or domain ontologies.

In particular, a benchmark dossier can disclose judge ancestry without this toolkit running benchmark tasks or scoring model performance. A dataset dossier can disclose common data origins without this toolkit measuring cross-generation support collapse.

### 3.3 Workflow boundary

The proposed future workflow is:

1. The caller declares an inquiry, target claim or claims, relevant artifact versions, time and system boundary.
2. The caller supplies structured records and supporting references, including explicit unknowns and disputes.
3. The toolkit performs structural validation and normalization while preserving assertion provenance.
4. Each requested analysis checks its own evidence prerequisites.
5. Eligible graph analyses derive paths, shared ancestry, scoped inventories and documented outcome relationships.
6. JSON and Markdown reports expose the findings, witness records, unavailable analyses and limitations.
7. Corrections to the dossier can produce a new audit snapshot without rewriting the earlier input or report.

Steps 3-6 describe planned capabilities after separate implementation authorization. Nothing in this delivery executes the workflow.

## 4. Inquiry, graph and time boundary

The unit of analysis is a **claim-scoped inquiry inside a declared audit snapshot**. A single bundle may contain several claims, but evidence roles and conclusions must remain separated by claim.

The inquiry must make the following concepts available. Exact input fields are in the accepted Work Unit 3 contract; this revision does not modify them:

| Context element | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| Target | The statement, answer, dataset claim or evaluative judgment being audited |
| Evidence role | What the artifact is evidence for in this inquiry |
| Version | Which claim and artifact versions the relationships concern |
| System boundary | Which generator, retriever, corpus, evaluation process or answer system defines internal and external for this audit |
| Temporal scope | Relevant observation, publication, receipt, review and effect times, with unknowns preserved |
| Relationship scope | Which typed relations can be used for the requested analysis |
| Coverage | Which records, upstream areas, actors or stages were examined, omitted, protected or left unresolved |
| Assertion basis | Who supplied a relationship, how it was obtained, and what evidence or attestation supports it |

An artifact can be an original record of what its author stated and remain derivative evidence about the underlying event. Two reports about the same event can contain separately documented collection processes. Two analyses of one dataset share the data-collection origin even where their methods differ. [SIT-D004; SIT-T020, SIT-T035-SIT-T036]

Coverage declarations must be narrow: a declared-complete citation list is not a declared-complete ownership history, and neither guarantees complete model-training ancestry. Completeness is itself an attributed assertion. It may support a result limited to that declared coverage; it does not certify completeness of the world.

## 5. Input contract and completeness

### 5.1 Proposed accepted modes

SIT-D016 proposes one canonical, versioned UTF-8 JSON bundle as the v0.1 interchange format, accepted from an explicitly selected local file. A future in-process library entry point accepts the same logical payload. It does not define a second set of semantics.

The accepted Work Unit 3 contract defines the logical `sit-bundle/0.1` envelope, reference rules and records through SIT-D019-SIT-D021. This revision uses that documentation-level contract and introduces no new JSON key, fixture, executable schema or Python API implementation.

Direct PDF, HTML, prose, browser, DOI-resolution, live URL, arbitrary CSV and JSON Lines ingestion are outside this release proposal. Another application may prepare the canonical bundle, but any extraction or inference it performs must remain attributed input to this toolkit. A citation locator is inert; its presence does not authorize fetching it.

### 5.2 Required record families

The input contract must support these conceptual record families. They are requirements for the ontology work, not a frozen list of classes or serialization keys.

| Family | Caller provides | Toolkit may later do |
|---|---|---|
| Inquiry and audit context | Target, boundary, versions, time and coverage | Validate structural coherence and attach scope to every result |
| Claims and evidence contributions | Stable references and what each contribution is offered to support, contradict or describe | Keep contributions claim-specific and preserve their epistemic role |
| Artifacts and actors | Versioned artifact references; published or protected actor references | Inventory supplied identities without declaring them authenticated |
| Origin and transformation assertions | Collection, documentary, analytical or other origin roles; typed derivation history | Trace permitted relationships and expose unresolved boundaries |
| Assertion evidence | Asserter, method, supporting record or protected attestation, qualifications | Distinguish supplied declarations and inferences from graph-derived results |
| Independence assessments | Comparison set, dependency dimensions, evidence and unexamined dimensions | Report qualified assessments and detect explicit incompatible ancestry |
| Evaluative processes | Candidate, generator, rubric, reference, judge and validation relationships | Disclose documented overlap by role and lineage dimension |
| External-input stages | Admission, preservation, selection and influence records where available | Report evidence separately for each stage |
| Correction and review | Objection or correction, target/version, route, authority, handling and outcome evidence | Distinguish declared route, applicable route, handling and downstream effect |
| Contestation and anomalies | Supplied disputes, original context or protected reference, intake/selection records | Preserve disagreements and assess recorded omissions against a defined set |

### 5.3 Three separate adequacy questions

**Structural adequacy.** The bundle must meet the eventual minimum envelope and reference rules. Duplicate conflicting identifiers, malformed types and impermissible relations cannot be silently repaired.

**Analytical adequacy.** A structurally valid bundle may have little upstream evidence. The toolkit must still be able to inventory it and report why stronger analyses are unavailable. Complete provenance is not a universal ingestion prerequisite.

**Substantive adequacy.** Well-formed records do not establish that the assertions about the world are correct. Structural acceptance cannot issue identity verification, evidentiary validation or truth certification.

### 5.4 Missing, disputed and protected records

The accepted Work Unit 3 contract represents a known gap through an explicit UnresolvedReference and rejects accidental dangling references as structural errors. This threat-capability revision preserves those exact rules.

Evidence that is unavailable, undisclosed, disputed or inapplicable must not be collapsed into a numerical zero. Conflicting, structurally valid assertions must remain available, with affected conclusions limited until a documented adjudication applies.

Opaque identifiers can preserve known common ancestry without public names. A protected reference supplies only the assurance actually documented by its attestation. Stable identifiers alone provide no independence proof. [SIT-D006, SIT-D007, SIT-D014]

The primary responsibility boundary is explicit:

|---|---|---|---|
| Citation | A cited relationship with an artifact reference | Citation inventory or cycle in the recorded citation graph | Automatic endorsement, derivation or semantic support |
| Common ancestry | Typed dependency assertions for a claim | Shared documented origin and witness paths | Proof that no undisclosed ancestor exists |
| Independent acquisition | A qualified assessment and its evidence | Independence result within the assessed dimension and comparison set | General statistical independence or a global independent-source label |
| Model overlap | Documented model, training-data or rubric lineage | Role-specific overlap and its record basis | Measured error correlation, hidden training disclosure or identical behavior |
| Presence | Origin and pipeline-stage evidence | Boundary-qualified external-input and stage profile | Adequate continuous reality contact from a snapshot alone |
| Correction | Route, authority, processing and linked version outcomes | Evidenced route or observed correction outcome | Execution of rollback, retrieval changes or deployment controls |
| Retention | A known eligible input set and selection records | Retained or excluded supplied items | Undocumented suppression or an actor's motive |
| Verification | A verification event with method, scope and authority | Display of that attributed verification | Creation of a verified or true badge by ingestion |

The toolkit may import an upstream investigator's inference as an attributed inference. It may not relabel it as a directly observed fact simply because the graph is internally consistent. Graph derivations must expose the assertions on which they depend. [SIT-D003, SIT-D007]

### 7.1 Required but evidence-conditional analyses

SIT-D017 proposes that common ancestry, scoped root resolution, evaluator overlap, correction paths and the evidence profiles above form the usable release core. A required analysis must provide either a supported result or its explicit unmet prerequisites. It cannot disappear merely because the record is difficult, and it cannot invent a value to fill the report.

Conditional output and optional functionality are different. The first describes an included analysis whose data requirements are not met. The second describes a feature not included unless separately selected. The accepted Work Unit 4 contract selects the restricted SIT-M005 scalar through SIT-D023. It must provide its eligible value or its explicit unmet prerequisites when implemented; it cannot silently disappear as an unimplemented optional module.

### 7.2 Origin and independence

A terminal graph node can represent a documented origin, a declared boundary or missing ancestry. Those meanings must remain distinguishable. The program cannot infer an original observation from the absence of a recorded parent.

Independence is assessed for a declared set and specified dependency dimensions, such as acquisition, method, model ancestry, rubric or organizational control. A known shared dataset can coexist with distinct analytical methods. Independence in one dimension cannot discharge unresolved dependence in another. Pairwise assessments cannot be silently made transitive or promoted to mutual statistical independence.

The accepted count, defined in SIT-M003 through SIT-D022, concerns the supplied and qualified comparison set. It does not purport to find the maximum number of independent sources in the world. Where qualification is insufficient, return the documented origins, relationships and gaps instead of an unqualified independent total. [SIT-D004-D006]

### 7.3 Concentration and derivative contribution

The required release core is the origin-incidence profile in SIT-M004: explicit seed contributions, their known origin sets, declared boundaries, scope cuts and unresolved/conflicted branches. A seed counts at most once for each origin, even when multiple paths reach it. Multi-origin incidence fractions overlap and are not normalized into contribution weights.

SIT-D023 proposes one supplementary scalar, `single_origin_contribution_hhi`, only when every seed in the full claim/dimension population resolves to exactly one documentary-qualified origin under the defined identity, coverage and time prerequisites. It uses one counting unit per supplied EvidenceItem and `sum((n_r/N)^2)`. The unit is represented contribution frequency, not evidence strength or independent information. Root-bucket independence requires its own supplied assessment.

One mixed, unresolved, declared-only or baseline-cut row makes this full-population scalar unavailable; the profile and full denominator remain visible. There is no resolved-only fallback, default equal parent split, path-count weighting or numerical policy extracted from narrative `portion_note` fields. The unchanged input contract contains no approved quantitative multi-parent allocation record.

Derivative contribution is reported through SIT-M006's explicitly labeled immediate-EvidenceItem-layer direct/inherited/mixed/unresolved profile. Its inherited-only point fraction requires a known nonzero denominator and no unresolved classifications. A finite-record completion interval can preserve unknown rows where the defined conditions hold. It does not estimate semantic novelty, loss of knowledge or independent acquisition.

General multi-parent weighted concentration, reciprocal/effective-root number, partial-provenance HHI bounds and high/low/extreme bands are outside the proposed v0.1 numerical contract. One concentrated origin does not establish falsehood or functional failure. Exact definitions and non-result rules are in definitions §§22-24 and reporting §§2-6. [SIT-T017, SIT-T037; SIT-D023]

### 7.4 Evaluators and judgment input

The audit must distinguish generator, reference answer, rubric, judge and downstream review roles even when one actor performs several roles. Documented common ancestry supports a scoped overlap finding. Different providers, endpoints or model names do not establish independence.

A human evaluator can have unresolved source or process dependencies. A machine execution can provide a different kind of external constraint. The report must preserve those distinctions without declaring either one universally independent or universally invalid. [SIT-D002, SIT-D005; EC, §§3.2, 6]

### 7.5 Presence and correction

Presence reporting separates admission, preservation, selection and downstream influence. Time and externality are relative to the inquiry. An archived original record can be relevant to a historical question; a recent derivative can carry no new observation. A single snapshot cannot establish continuous renewal, adequate replenishment or a causal elasticity. [HDL, §4.6; SIT-D008]

Correction reporting separately addresses a declared route, an authorized applicable route, observed handling and documented downstream effect. Missing capacity estimates do not erase an individually observed correction. A successful single correction does not demonstrate unlimited or sustained capacity.

A mailbox can show a contact route. A reasoned rejection can show handling. Neither establishes that the objection was correct. A later edit requires supporting linkage before it can be attributed to that correction. The toolkit audits these records; it never performs the intervention. [EC, §7.5; SIT-D009]

### 7.6 Anomalies, disputes and stage retention

Unclassified anomalies must retain context or a privacy-compatible reference. Supplied disagreement must survive normalization and reporting. Retaining a claim does not endorse it or give every source equal evidentiary weight.

Measuring retention requires an eligible input universe or an explicitly recorded stage comparison. A final bundle with no intake history cannot prove suppression merely because it contains no contradiction. [EC, §§7.1, 7.4; HDL, §9.7; SIT-D013]

## 8. Report and interface contract

SIT-D018 proposes a local command-line interface and an in-process Python library interface over the same analysis contract. Exact command names, function names and package layout are reserved for architecture work. Nothing here creates or exposes an API.

The two required report formats are machine-readable JSON and human-readable Markdown. HTML, dashboards, browser extensions and hosted services are deferred. The Markdown report must carry the same substantive limitations and evidence status as the JSON report.

### 8.1 Required report content

| Section | Required content |
|---|---|
| Run and input identity | Tool and contract versions once defined; supplied input identity; configuration; audit time/context; stated scope and coverage |
| Validation and capabilities | Structural problems, available analyses, unmet prerequisites and analyses not performed |
| Artifact and origin inventory | Counted units, versions, claim roles, documented origins and unresolved boundaries |
| Lineage and independence | Permitted relationship types, witness paths, comparison sets, independence assertions and contrary or missing evidence |
| Evaluator profile | Role-specific shared ancestry and unresolved dimensions |
| Presence profile | External-origin basis and separate evidence for the four replenishment stages |
| Correction profile | Target, route, authority, handling, propagation and linked outcomes |
| Contestation and retention | Preserved disputes/anomalies, eligible comparison sets and observed exclusions |
| Limits and evidence trail | Assertion references, assurance limits, protected information and conclusions the audit cannot support |

Each finding must identify its claim or target, artifact versions, evidence basis, derivation rule, coverage and limitations. Human-readable explanations use deterministic, rule-bound text in the proposed release; a generative model is not required to narrate the report.

### 8.2 Result states

The future contract must distinguish at least the meanings of: supported structural result, evidence unavailable, inapplicable analysis, disputed basis, invalid input and analysis not performed. The final output enum vocabulary belongs to Work Unit 6; WU3 input enums are separate. Definitions §28.2 now specifies the analytical non-result meanings without freezing their serialization.

A valid inventory count of zero has a defined counted set. It cannot be substituted for an unavailable independent-root count. No recorded path in a partial graph has a different meaning from no path within explicitly declared complete relevant coverage.

### 8.3 Determinism and artifact handling

The same normalized input, contract version and configuration must produce the same substantive results and stable ordering. Run timestamps and other volatile envelope metadata must remain separate from substantive comparisons. No random tie-breaking or remote lookup may change the audit silently.

Input and reference artifacts are read-only. Report output requires an explicit local destination and must not overwrite source records or protected earlier reports silently. Exact overwrite, redaction and atomic-write controls are assigned to the data, privacy and architecture specifications.

## 9. Required specification examples

These are product counterexamples, not final fixtures, executable tests or golden-number contracts. Work Unit 7 owns the canonical hero and exact expected outputs.

| Case | Required distinction | Forbidden conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Five documents explicitly derive the same claim from one documented measurement origin | Five artifacts and one recorded measurement lineage | Five independent observations, or automatically false evidence |
| Two observations of one event have separate acquisition records and a disclosed shared calibration | Acquisition evidence and shared calibration are both visible | Independence in every dimension because two instruments exist |
| One document copies claim C1 and contributes a new interview for C2 | Different claim-relative roles inside one artifact | One global independent/derivative label |
| Three upstream references are unresolved | Three observed unresolved references with unknown hidden multiplicity | Three known hidden origins or three independent roots |

## 10. Exclusions and deferred features

### 10.1 Prohibited v0.1 claims

The release must not provide a universal source-integrity, truth, credibility or model-safety score. It must not infer truth from institutional status, source count, consensus, recency, human origin, model origin or graph shape. It must not produce ideological rankings or recommendations about political choices.

It must not describe topological concentration as effective independent sample size, measure correlated error from family overlap alone, claim mutual information from a source graph, or report collapse probability from generic entropy language.

### 10.2 Outside the release implementation

Live crawling, hidden-ancestry discovery, automatic identity resolution, semantic claim verification, model-based judging, embeddings and near-duplicate detection are outside this proposal. Imported evidence concerning such investigations remains attributed data.

The full SIL registry, trust-weighted retrieval, source sanctions, appeals service, publisher compensation and crawler governance are outside the release. Existing records about them may be represented where relevant to an audit. No network opt-in mode, plugins or automatic intervention path is included by leaving an extension field available.

Active prompt-injection, poisoning and manipulation detectors remain outside v0.1 under the accepted SIT-D003/SIT-D017 direction; WU5 makes that boundary explicit through proposed SIT-D027 without selecting an optional content detector. Structural input defenses and treating all content as inert data remain required. Representing a supplied incident does not mean the toolkit detected it.

Recursive-resampling simulations, longitudinal model-collapse analysis, semantic-gradient experiments, queueing models and whole-system deployment certification remain separate research or product work.

### 10.3 Explicit later decisions

The exact dependency dimensions, input object names and documentary qualification are in the accepted WU3 contract. WU4's restricted analytical catalog, denominator rules and explicit scalar exclusions are accepted through SIT-D022-SIT-D025. WU5 submits threat meanings and self-protection boundaries through SIT-D026-SIT-D027. Status vocabulary and full observability levels remain WU6 work; resource/security limits remain WU9; module/interface/dependency details remain WU10. These bounded tasks do not permit defaults to be invented during implementation. A required capability that cannot be specified at its gate must remain held or be explicitly removed by the owner.

## 11. Privacy, safety and product separation

The proposed operational baseline has no network fetching, telemetry, uploads, model-provider calls or remote identity verification. Source text and metadata are untrusted data. Embedded instructions cannot execute code, change the analysis policy, remove warnings or create new write authority.

The release must support protected identifiers and avoid mandatory public source names. Exact protection, export and retention policies belong to Work Unit 9. No promise of cryptographic attestation verification, automatic personal-data detection or legal compliance is made here.

Source Integrity Toolkit and Recursive Integrity Toolkit remain independent projects. Their use of provenance methods may overlap. These responsibility lists do not assign exclusive ownership of graph algorithms or alter Recursive Integrity Toolkit's approved scope. No internal imports, shared package, schema compatibility or migration is assumed. Future interoperability requires an explicit versioned mapping that preserves scope, assertion provenance and uncertainty. [SIT-D015]

Software, reusable specifications, fixtures and source papers require separately recorded license treatment in Work Unit 9. No license from another project is adopted by this document. No new GitHub repository or remote change is included in this work unit.
